# CMMC Phase II Suspension — Verified Facts (July 13, 2026)

## Sources
- DoD/DoW press release: war.gov, July 13, 2026
- SBA press release: sba.gov, Release 26-73, July 13, 2026
- Federal News Network article (verified against both primary sources)
- Crowell & Moring legal analysis (cross-referenced)

## What Was Suspended
- CMMC Level 2 (C3PAO) third-party certification assessments
- CMMC Level 3 (DIBCAC) government-led assessments
- November 10, 2026 Phase 2 transition deadline
- All new C3PAO/DIBCAC milestones in DoW contracts
- Waiver procedures

## What Remained in Force
- DFARS 252.204-7012: Safeguarding CDI and Cyber Incident Reporting — UNCHANGED
- CMMC Level 1 (Self) assessments — UNCHANGED
- CMMC Level 2 (Self) assessments — UNCHANGED and now the PRIMARY enforcement mechanism
- NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2 all 110 controls — UNCHANGED
- SPRS scoring and submission requirements — UNCHANGED
- DOJ Civil Cyber-Fraud Initiative (False Claims Act) — UNCHANGED

## Key Numbers (from SBA Release 26-73)
- ~120,000 small businesses in DIB impacted
- ~$593,800 per C3PAO certification for small firms
- ~$388,600 per self-assessment for small firms
- ~100 C3PAO assessors available (1,200:1 ratio vs. 120K businesses)
- 60-day reform task force; report due ~September 11, 2026

## Market Impact
- CMMC compliance consulting: $1.8-2.5B (shifted from C3PAO prep to self-assessment enablement)
- CMMC compliance SaaS: $2.1B, growing at 15.8% CAGR
- Less than 15% of DIB contractors fully compliant with NIST 800-171
- Self-assessment is now the primary enforcement mechanism during review period

## Strategic Implications
- Build products for NIST 800-171 compliance (stable), not CMMC branding (volatile)
- Self-assessment enablement is the near-term market
- Primes still demand subcontractor compliance (DFARS 7012 unchanged)
- Task force reform likely simplifies but does not eliminate cybersecurity requirements
- Window: 60 days where competitors may freeze while demand continues

## Verified Quote
From SBA Administrator Loeffler: "With over 100,000 small businesses impacted and compliance costs approaching as much as $600,000, the SBA strongly supports the Department of War's decisive action."
