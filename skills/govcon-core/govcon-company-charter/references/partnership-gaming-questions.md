# Partnership Gaming Questions Framework

Reusable strategic question framework for evaluating any GovCon partnership. Apply these when a partner sends their charter, capability statement, or partnership proposal. Not every question applies to every partnership — select the 5-7 most relevant.

## Core Structure Questions

1. **Equal partners or prime/sub?** Does the partner's document position you as co-equal, optional add-on, or subordinate? Does the operational reality (what you've already done together) match the document's voice? If not, the gap is a power dynamic signal.

2. **Where is the equity/ownership conversation?** Has anyone discussed who owns joint product lines, IP developed together, or customer relationships? If both parties are avoiding this, surface it before more joint work is done.

3. **Who's the CEO of the combined entity?** In a joint capture, who makes the final call on pricing, technical architecture, go/no-go, and hiring? If leadership is ambiguous, recommend domain-based decision rights.

## Partner Assessment Questions

4. **What does [Partner B] actually want?** If the partner has multiple members/principals, have you interacted with ALL of them? The one you talk to most may not represent the others. Watch for engagement signals in meetings: does the quieter partner ask substantive questions, push back, or defer completely?

5. **What's the partner's employer situation?** Are they still employed by the company whose past performance they're citing? Non-compete risk, reputation risk, and timeline risk all flow from this. The charter should not over-cite an employer both parties may need distance from.

## Competitive Positioning Questions

6. **Can you deliver independently?** If you could deliver the product/service without the partner, what's their irreducible contribution? Articulate it explicitly in the joint value proposition — otherwise primes will ask.

7. **Build together or build alone?** If the partner's vision for a key product line (e.g., a GCC High enclave) is 5-10× overbuilt vs. what you can deliver with existing partnerships (e.g., PreVeil reseller), the gap isn't just tactical — it's philosophical. Surface it before it becomes a conflict.

## Narrative Tension Questions

8. **Are you telling the same story?** Compare your partnership proposal against their charter. If yours says "services-to-products company" and theirs says "practitioner-led consulting firm," those are two different businesses. Reconcile before sharing externally.

9. **What does their charter get right that your plan misses?** Be honest. Partner charters often have strengths (member depth, legal grounding, domain-specific differentiation) that your plan lacks. Acknowledge them — it builds trust and improves the joint offering.

## Pre-Meeting Questions (for the founder)

10. **Revenue split: what's your number?** If you don't have one before the meeting, the partner will propose theirs — and you'll be reacting, not shaping.

11. **Are you willing to be "optional" in their document?** If yes, optionality is the strategy. If no, push for symmetric positioning in the next revision.

12. **What's the one thing you won't compromise on?** IP ownership? Productization methodology? Decision rights? Pricing? Know it before you walk in.

13. **Should there be a joint charter?** Two separate charters with cross-references works for teaming. For joint capture, a single combined document may be more effective. Decide before the meeting.
