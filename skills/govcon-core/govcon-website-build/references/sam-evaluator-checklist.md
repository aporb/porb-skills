# SAM.gov Evaluator Checklist — What Gets Verified on the Public Website

When a GovCon firm submits a response to a Sources Sought, RFI, or RFP, SAM.gov and
contracting officers will cross-reference the company's public website against the
SAM registration. These are the items evaluators look for and verify.

**The public site and SAM registration must be consistent.** Discrepancies trigger
questions and can delay or disqualify.

## The 14 Checklist Items

| # | Item | What to Verify |
|---|------|---------------|
| 1 | **Company name + entity type** | Legal name matches SAM.gov exactly. "Leatherneck Federal Consulting LLC," not "LFC" or "LFC Federal." Entity type: SC LLC. |
| 2 | **UEI, CAGE** | Same numbers as SAM registration. UEI + CAGE should appear in the contact section. EIN is sensitive — do NOT publish it on the public site. |
| 3 | **Key personnel listed** | Names, titles, certs, and bios match SAM.gov Points of Contact. ⚠ **Conflict:** if named personnel hold day jobs, keep bios off the public site and in a private capabilities statement PDF shared directly. |
| 4 | **Contact info** | Phone number and email match SAM.gov. Consistent across site and registration. |
| 5 | **Address** | Physical address matches SAM.gov entity record exactly. |
| 6 | **NAICS codes** | All registered NAICS codes displayed. Primary code flagged. Must match SAM.gov. |
| 7 | **SDVOSB / socio-economic status** | Status accurately stated. If self-attested only, say "self-attested." If VetCert in process, say so — never claim certified until approved. |
| 8 | **Capabilities match** | Services described on the site align with NAICS codes and the work the firm actually bids. Any capability listed on the site should be defensible in an oral presentation. |
| 9 | **No unrelated entity mentions** | Separate legal entities (HARBOR Initiative LLC, Aecon, etc.) must not appear as if they are the same company. |
| 10 | **No current-employer references** | If principals hold day jobs (e.g., at Aecon), their current employer must not be named in bios or JSON-LD on the public site. |
| 11 | **No third-party IP claims** | Products owned by other entities (AI-DDSF, Navaide) must not be claimed as the firm's own. Can mention team experience generically. |
| 12 | **Federal experience highlighted** | Agencies and programs the team has served: DAF, USMC, Navy, DOE, SRNS, GE Aviation, etc. Aggregate, no individual names needed. |
| 13 | **New firm transparency** | If the entity is newly formed (2026), be transparent about formation date while highlighting combined team experience. "Formed May 2026, built on 60+ years combined federal acquisition leadership." |
| 14 | **Certification status transparency** | If SBA VetCert or other certifications are pending, state that clearly. "VetCert application in process" is honest and evaluators respect transparency. |

## The Named-Personnel Tension

The #3 item is the hardest constraint. Evaluators want to see named bios to verify
the team matches the SAM.gov Points of Contact. But named bios on the public website
create day-job conflict-of-interest exposure and get crawled by Google.

**Standard resolution:** the public site has anonymous/aggregate leadership (credential
pillars, combined stats, no photos, no names). Named bios live in a **private
capabilities-statement PDF** that is shared directly with evaluators and contracting
officers — not indexed, not crawled, not a public URL.
